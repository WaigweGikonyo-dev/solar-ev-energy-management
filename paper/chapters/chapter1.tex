\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background of the Study}

The global transition toward sustainable transportation has accelerated the adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) as a key strategy for reducing carbon emissions and dependence on fossil fuels. In Africa, and particularly in Kenya, this transition is emerging concurrently with strong growth in renewable energy infrastructure, especially geothermal, wind, and solar photovoltaic (PV) systems.

Kenya presents a unique energy context. While the national grid benefits from a relatively high renewable energy share compared to global averages, distribution-level reliability remains inconsistent, particularly in peri-urban and rural areas. These weaknesses introduce challenges for continuous and reliable EV charging infrastructure deployment.

Solar PV systems are highly suitable for Kenya due to its high solar radiation levels throughout the year. However, PV systems are intermittent and require integration with energy storage systems and grid support mechanisms to ensure stable operation.

In practical deployments, especially in weak-grid environments, diesel generators are still used as backup sources to guarantee uninterrupted EV charging services. While effective for reliability, this introduces significant fuel costs and increases carbon emissions, partially offsetting the environmental benefits of EV adoption.

---

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Despite Kenya’s strong renewable energy potential, the deployment of reliable and sustainable EV charging infrastructure is constrained by three interconnected challenges:

1. Grid instability and localized power interruptions in distribution networks  
2. Intermittent nature of solar PV generation  
3. Continued reliance on diesel generators as backup power sources  

Existing energy management strategies for EV charging systems typically assume stable grid conditions or do not jointly optimize energy cost, reliability, fuel consumption, and carbon emissions.

There is therefore a need for an integrated adaptive energy management system capable of dynamically coordinating solar PV generation, battery storage, grid supply, and diesel backup under realistic Kenyan operating conditions.

---

\subsection{Research Questions}

This study is guided by the following primary research question:

\textbf{How can an adaptive energy management system optimize solar-assisted EV charging operations in Kenya under weak-grid conditions while minimizing fuel consumption and carbon emissions?}

---

\subsection{Objectives of the Study}

The main objective of this study is to design and evaluate an adaptive energy management system for solar-assisted EV charging stations operating under weak-grid conditions in Kenya.

The specific objectives are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item To develop a mathematical model of a hybrid energy system consisting of solar PV generation, battery energy storage, grid supply, and diesel backup.
    
    \item To model EV charging demand profiles representative of Kenyan urban and peri-urban mobility patterns.
    
    \item To design an adaptive energy management strategy that dynamically allocates power between renewable, storage, grid, and backup sources.
    
    \item To minimize diesel fuel consumption while ensuring continuous EV charging service reliability.
    
    \item To evaluate and quantify carbon emissions under different energy dispatch strategies.
    
    \item To simulate system performance under realistic Kenyan solar irradiance and grid reliability conditions.
\end{itemize}

---

\subsection{Scope of the Study}

This study focuses on grid-connected hybrid EV charging stations in Kenya with integrated solar PV, battery storage, and diesel backup systems. The analysis considers variability in solar irradiance, EV charging demand, and grid reliability.

The findings are intended to be extendable to other Sub-Saharan African countries with similar energy infrastructure constraints, including intermittent grid reliability, high solar potential, and emerging EV adoption.

---

\subsection{Significance of the Study}

This research contributes to the development of sustainable EV charging infrastructure in Kenya by addressing both technical and environmental constraints. The proposed system aims to improve energy reliability while reducing dependence on fossil fuels and minimizing carbon emissions.

The study is significant for policymakers, energy planners, and infrastructure developers seeking to integrate renewable energy into transportation systems in weak grid environments.

---

\subsection{Expected Contribution}

This study contributes:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A system-level model for hybrid solar-EV charging under weak-grid conditions.
    \item An adaptive energy management framework for real-time energy allocation.
    \item A quantitative assessment of fuel and emissions reduction strategies.
    \item A simulation framework applicable to Kenyan and similar African energy contexts.
\end{itemize}
